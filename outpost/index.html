    How do we create something memorable and realistic?

    We had a thought about what might make this assignment unforgettable.

    One of the difficulties with AI generated content is that it feels OVERLY polished and OVERLY cinematic.

    When most productions set out this is what they try to achieve, but it doesn’t carry the weight of REALITY or the verisimilitude that comes with.

    In reality, demonstration videos of aerospace technology are typically cramped, a bit messy and done with non-cinematic instrumentation.

    reference: SpaceX / Starship ocean landing, drone view

    The camera is part of the event.

    Using the recently viral SpaceX splashdown as a jumping-off point, we thought: what if we created video that felt indistinguishable from current aerospace use case videos? The drone is trying to keep up. Its propellers get into the frame. For a moment, the camera loses the thing it came to film. Those messy details put us in the scene and make it feel REAL.

    We’d like to bring that feeling to Outpost: a remarkable operation, seen from a camera that could actually be there.

    Check every clip.

    A good first frame is only the beginning. The vehicle still has to hold together as it moves.

    We compare the full clip with the approved storyboard and model views, looking for changing shapes, drifting markings and unclear action.

    We regenerate or correct problem areas, and shorten a shot when that makes the operation clearer.

    The sort of detail we need to protect: a fixed shape, a clear connection, an action we can follow.

    reference: NASA JPL Perseverance / real footage illustrating hardware and connection, not a composite

    Let the camera miss a little.

    Water reaches the lens. Exhaust hides the landing. A window catches the sun.

    These details work because something in the scene causes them. We build them around the action, then check that you can still follow what happened.

    references: NASA recovery rehearsal / SpaceX SN15 landing / NASA Orion window camera

    Make one scene first.

    A short test built around one of the harder actions in the brief. About 10–15 seconds is enough to see where the method holds up.

    We’d bring back:

    • A storyboard made with your 3D assets.
    • Camera-look tests, with and without LoRAs.
    • One assembled clip, with sound.

    We’d review it together:

    • Does the vehicle stay right?
    • Can we follow the operation?
    • Does it feel like a camera was there?

    Then we can agree the approach, scope and schedule from something we’ve actually seen.
